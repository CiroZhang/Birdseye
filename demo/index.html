Self-Detected End-to-End 3D Reconstruction
and Analysis of Badminton Rallies

Ciro Zhang · Harvard University

A monocular badminton analysis pipeline in which every stage is driven by our own detectors, with no manual annotation anywhere upstream of the hit-timing labels needed for supervision. Court geometry is self-calibrated against detected (not annotated) court points; 3D player pose comes from our own detection-plus-reconstruction stack; a transformer trained on this self-detected motion alone reaches 92.7% F1 for hit detection, exceeding published baselines.

Real-time pose detection on rally footage

YOLOv8-pose run directly on raw BFMD broadcast video — officials and spectators filtered out (kept only the two largest, closest detections per frame), uniform single-color skeleton rendering. Synced against our self-detected 3D reconstruction and the hit-timing transformer's predicted shot type, on four real professional matches.

Figure 1. Left: our transformer's per-frame 3D skeleton reconstruction, orbit-able. Right: the same rally's real broadcast footage with YOLOv8-pose keypoints overlaid live. Use the dropdown to switch between four different matches.

Self-calibrated court & 3D player reconstruction

Independent validation against a real two-camera (top + back view) doubles drone dataset with hand-annotated ground truth, not used anywhere else in this work. Green dots are the real annotated player positions; red dots are our self-detected reconstruction, reprojected back into the top-view frame.

Figure 2. Top view + back view + orbitable 3D skeleton reconstruction, played back in sync across all four doubles players.

Shuttle trajectory reconstruction

Physics-based 3D fit of the shuttlecock's flight per shot, synced against the source broadcast footage. This is an early-stage extension to the pipeline above and is not yet validated against ground truth — included here to show current direction, not a finished result.
