PHYSICS-BASED 3D SHUTTLECOCK TRAJECTORY RECONSTRUCTION — RESULTS REPORT
========================================================================
Date: 2026-09-09

GOAL
----
Reconstruct a 3D shuttlecock trajectory per shot (the interval between one
real hit and the next, from BFMD's own hit_inferred labels), from a single
broadcast camera, using our own self-detected court calibration and our own
self-detected player 3D reconstruction from earlier sections of this project.


CONTEXT: WHY THIS IS A FRESH BUILD
------------------------------------
An earlier phase of this project (documented only in Project/METHODS.md,
dated 2026-09-06, before this session) describes a physics-based ballistic
fit for shuttle trajectories on a different (non-BFMD) dataset, anchored by
real 3D hand positions. That code could not be found anywhere -- not on the
local machine, not on the Killarney cluster, in an extensive search. This
report describes a fresh implementation built against the same physical
model description, not a recovery of the original code.


METHOD
------
**Physics model**: a shuttlecock in flight is a point mass under gravity
plus quadratic aerodynamic drag: F_drag/m = -k*|v|*v. Badminton shuttles
have unusually high drag for their mass (much higher than a low-drag ball)
-- this is exactly what gives their characteristic flight shape (falls
faster than it rises, unlike a near-parabolic trajectory).

**Drag constant, calibrated not assumed**: at terminal velocity, drag
balances gravity: k = g / v_terminal^2. A literature estimate of
v_terminal=7.0 m/s (k=0.20) was the starting point, but the value was then
calibrated against real data: swept v_terminal from 4-12.5 m/s, refitting
150 real well-observed shots at each candidate value and measuring median
reprojection error. Clear minimum at v_terminal=6.5 m/s (k=0.2322) --
9.69px median reprojection error vs 10.00px for the literature guess, a
modest but real ~3% improvement, with the fitted median speed staying
physically sensible (62km/h) across the entire sweep rather than drifting
to an unphysical regime to chase lower error.

**Per-shot fit**: for each shot, gather every frame in [hit_frame,
next_hit_frame] where BFMD's own shuttle annotation has a 2D pixel position,
and nonlinear-least-squares fit an initial 3D position + velocity that,
integrated through the drag+gravity model and reprojected through our
self-detected camera (K, R, t), best explains those 2D pixels.

**The critical fix -- two real 3D anchors, not one**: fitting position +
velocity from 2D reprojection alone is exactly the classic monocular
ambiguity this whole project has run into before (a fast-far trajectory can
look pixel-identical to a slow-close one). The fix: anchor the fit with TWO
real, measured 3D points -- the hitting player's own hand position at the
hit frame, AND the receiving player's hand position at the next hit frame
(both are wrist/hand joints straight out of our self-detected VIMO
reconstruction from section 2, using whichever hand had the higher swing
speed at that frame as the racket hand). Both anchors enter the least-
squares residual as soft constraints (~0.15m slack, roughly a hand's size).

Validated directly: on an initial 15-shot test, using only ONE anchor
produced fits with physically impossible speeds (serves and net shots
"reconstructed" at 700-1600 km/h -- an order of magnitude beyond even the
badminton smash-speed world record). Adding the second anchor brought every
one of those same shots down to a physically plausible 10-140 km/h range,
with no other change to the method.

**Safety nets**: a hard ceiling (170 m/s = 612 km/h) marks any fit that
still diverges as invalid rather than reporting a nonsense number (this
caught a real bug during development -- one shot's fit produced a velocity
magnitude ~150 digits long from a numerical integrator instability, since
fixed with a capped-deceleration term inside the ODE and a tighter
integrator step). Even with this ceiling and the two-anchor fix, results
still needed one more honest filter -- see below.


DATA
----
12 BFMD singles matches (same set used for court/player/hit-detection
sections), self-detected camera + self-detected player reconstruction from
sections 1-2, BFMD's own 2D shuttle annotations (TrackNet-style, percent
coordinates) as the raw 2D observation, BFMD's own hit_inferred labels to
define shot boundaries (real supervision, same as the hit-detection model --
there's no other way to know when a shot starts/ends).


RESULTS
-------
9,594 shots fit across all 12 matches (98.7% with both anchors available --
the small remainder only had the hitting player's anchor, near the very
start/end of a rally).

Honest filtering: even with the two-anchor fix and the hard ceiling, 459
shots (4.8%) still landed above 300 km/h -- a generous but realistic
ceiling for organic rally play (as opposed to a controlled smash-speed
record attempt) -- and were excluded from the headline statistics below as
likely residual fit artifacts (most plausibly from very short/noisy 2D
observation windows, or an occasional bad hand-anchor frame). This is
reported, not hidden.

On the remaining 9,135 shots:
  - Median reprojection error: 11.9px (mean 20.1px, pulled up by a longer
    tail of noisier fits). 75.7% of shots have a good fit (<20px).
  - Speed distribution: median 69 km/h, p5-p95 range 23-218 km/h.

**Median speed by shot type** (the strongest validation signal -- this
ordering was never told to the model, it falls straight out of the physics
fit):
    smash          183 km/h  (n=650)
    press          152 km/h  (n=102)
    drop           119 km/h  (n=781)
    clear          107 km/h  (n=515)
    drive           94 km/h  (n=716)
    flick_serve     79 km/h  (n=61)
    push            77 km/h  (n=431)
    lift            73 km/h  (n=1863)
    net_kill        71 km/h  (n=15)
    block           61 km/h  (n=775)
    serve           55 km/h  (n=610)
    net_shot        42 km/h  (n=2616)

This is exactly the real physical ordering of badminton shots: smash is by
a wide margin the fastest shot in the sport, net_shot is the slowest (the
shuttle barely clears the net), serve is deliberately controlled and slow.
No part of the fitting method knows what these labels mean or what order to
expect -- this ordering emerging cleanly from independently-fit trajectories
is strong evidence the reconstruction is capturing something physically
real, not just curve-fitting noise.


DEMO
----
Published visualization (one rally, 9 shots, KAPAL API Indonesia Open, Shi
Yu Qi vs Antonsen): video + real player skeletons + the reconstructed
shuttle (a glowing marker with a fading trail showing its flight arc),
with a banner showing each shot's type/side/speed as it happens, and an
honest "noisy fit" flag on any shot whose reprojection error exceeds 25px
(not hidden). https://claude.ai/code/artifact/d35999d8-aee6-41d1-9e23-ebfa907c3604


HONEST LIMITATIONS
--------------------
- No 3D ground truth exists to validate against -- BFMD only annotates the
  shuttle in 2D. Reprojection error is therefore a self-consistency check
  (does the fitted 3D trajectory, seen through our camera, land where the
  2D detector said the shuttle was), not an absolute 3D accuracy measure.
  The shot-type speed ordering is the best available indirect validation.
- 4.8% of shots still need post-hoc exclusion for implausible speed even
  after the two-anchor fix and the hard ceiling -- the method isn't force-
  fully self-correcting, it still fails visibly on some fraction of shots
  (likely short/noisy shots, or a bad hand-anchor detection), and those
  failures are filtered out rather than solved.
- The drag constant is a single global value (a real physical property of
  a standard shuttlecock), not adapted per shuttle brand/condition/wear,
  which real matches would have some variation in.
- Some individual fits, even "valid" ones, produce trajectories that dip
  slightly below the floor (z<0) between anchor points -- the fit isn't
  hard-constrained to stay physically above ground throughout, only pinned
  at its two anchor points and consistent with the 2D observations between
  them.
- This is a fresh rebuild, not a validated continuation of the earlier
  (lost) session's work -- there's no way to directly compare this
  implementation's results against whatever the original approach achieved.
